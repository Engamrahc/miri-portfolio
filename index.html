MIRI

A smart buddy for bridging the intention–action gap.

MIRI pairs a Flutter app that classifies how you're spending your time, entirely on-device, with a small desk companion that shows the result as an animated face. Built by a team of three at the University of the Cordilleras, Baguio City. I led the software and machine learning side.

How it works

Classifier

A small neural network trained on seven features, tuned with Bayesian optimization and scaled with RobustScaler. I chose it over simpler baselines because it fits edge deployment well once quantized, which the baseline comparisons in the thesis support. An out-of-distribution guard handles inputs unlike the training data.

Train/serve skew

Found and fixed a skew in the session-duration feature between training and live serving by holding that feature at its training median in the live path.

App

Riverpod state management, Firestore sync, background work through WorkManager, and a Reflect tab with a custom-painted usage donut chart. Smoothing uses an exponential moving average with hysteresis so the Buddy doesn't flicker between states. I also removed per-app package names from Firestore as a privacy fix and added ProGuard/R8 release rules.

Buddy hardware

An ESP32-S3 device receives state over BLE and renders an emotion on a TFT face and LED ring. The firmware face matches the app's painter style: ratio-based drawing, shared color palette, and bezier mouth curves. The team also produced a hardware documentation suite covering wiring, soldering, and enclosure drawings (203.2 × 101.6 × 100 mm box, PLA spine).

What I learned

  • Reporting cross-validated results next to test accuracy, and checking the quantized model against the full-precision one.
  • Finding bugs that only appear between training and serving, not inside either one.
  • Being honest about limits: some classifier states are hard to reach in live use and the hardware has had limited live state-change testing. I documented those openly rather than hiding them.
  • Working with AI coding tools productively by verifying output against the existing codebase instead of trusting it blindly.
